## 2 · F117 — the change that decides the patch

`Code/Fix_ArrivalDeaths.lua:201` calls `ChooseDome(self.traits, domes, false, dome_elevators)`.
That is 1.0.7's contract. 1.1.0 takes **the colonist** (`_GameUtils.lua:486`) and
both callees index `.traits` off the argument, so our traits table hands them
`nil` and the first trait-filtered dome or filtered residence with free space
**throws inside the arriving colonist's `Idle` command thread**. Full derivation:
`HOTFIX_2_AUDIT.md` §1 F-1 and `bugs/F117.md`.

### ⛔ The trap, and it is the whole difficulty

**Do NOT pass `self` on both branches unguarded.** ck118 binds. On a 1.0.7 body
`GetScoreFor(traits)` would receive a colonist object and **mis-score every
dome** — `colonist[attrib]` is nil for every trait. That is not a throw; it is
silently wrong, which is worse, and it is the F114 failure mode in reverse.

**So the branch must be decided by BEHAVIOUR, read from a callee body at call
time.** Never a version label, never `lua_revision` (`EF-077`: our revision and
both of 1.1.0's minimums are all 350453 — there is nothing to read).

### The design, as sketched — yours to finish, not to copy

The shape change is visible only at **call** time: `ChooseDome`'s own body did
not change, its callers did. And `Community:GetScoreFor` needs `g_Consts`, a
per-game GameVar, so an **apply-time** probe cannot run it.

⇒ a **one-shot probe inside the wrapper, on first use**: drive
`Community.GetScoreFor` on a stub community (`HasLifeSupport`, a one-key
`traits_filter`, empty residences) with a stub argument whose `traits` field is a
**capture table**. 1.1.0 indexes the capture (`colonist.traits[attrib]`); 1.0.7
indexes the stub itself (`traits[attrib]`). Cache the verdict.

⛔ **UNKNOWN is not permission.** Filed under neither shape, or it threw ⇒ **fall
back to the shipped behaviour (skip the re-choose)**, do not guess. Route it
through `SMRFixPack.Require`'s `probe` form so the `pcall` trap, the strict-`true`
rule and the decline logging are the shared ones (`FIX_POLICY` §2a), not
re-implemented here.

⚠️ **Establish the stub contract from the shipped body before you call it**, the
way `Fix_SaintBlessing.lua:156-190` does: show that the call is synchronous,
side-effect-free, touches no real object, and starts nothing. §2a requires it,
and it is what makes a stub call safe rather than merely convenient.

### ⛔ The falsifier — non-negotiable

**Every new inference ships with a falsifier, and the leg must be seen to FIRE
and seen NOT to fire.** Drive your discriminator against **both** shipped bodies
on a desk harness (both trees are on disk) and show it answering 1.1.0 on the
1.1.0 body and 1.0.7 on the 1.0.7 body. ⛔ **A leg that passes for the wrong
reason is worse than no leg** — that is exactly how F114 shipped, and a
falsifier that "passed while proving nothing" already cost this project a day on
09-09.

Update `bugs/F117.md` with what you built and what you did **not** establish.
⛔ **The status word does not move**: the repair is source-derived, like the
defect. The control in `F117.md` needs a colony and belongs to the sitting.

## 7 · Committing — the sharp one

⛔ **THE GIT INDEX AND HEAD ARE SHARED.** Peers work in this worktree; their
staged work sits in your index and their commits move your HEAD under you. A
bare `git commit`, a `git add -A`, **or even a directory pathspec** sweeps their
work into your commit — both have happened here.

⇒ **always an explicit list of individual FILE paths, on `add` AND on `commit`.**
`git commit -F <file>` (PowerShell splits `-m` on embedded quotes), then
**push** — pushing is standing-allowed. ⚠️ A `git status` pre-check cannot close
the race; treat an unexpected `LF will be replaced by CRLF` warning naming a file
you never touched as a **collision alarm**, not noise.

⚠️ **Do NOT compose code, prompts or regexes inside a bash heredoc.** Even a
quoted `<<'EOF'` eats one backslash level here — it has put a control byte in a
prompt and inverted a technical claim in a report. Use `Write`/`Edit`.
