### C47 — Open Farm plants 6.7× faster than its sibling on half the seed buffer, so it starves in a loop next to a full depot  `[filed 2026-08-15 from an organic owner observation during a terraforming speed-run — SOURCE-VERIFIED at Src, ⛔ NOT measured in game by us, ⛔ no fix proposed, ⛔ not a release gate. Owner rider: they are testing further themselves before launch]`

## The observation (owner, organic, 2026-08-15)

Speed-running terraforming, the owner hit **endless `Building is waiting for 1
Seeds to resume working` popups with their voiceovers** on an Open Farm. Their
words: *"The open farm seems to use seeds too rapidly and has such a small
internal storage that it's constantly out of materials, even though there is a
full depot beside it and drones seem to be constantly filling it up."*

Screenshots confirm the state rather than the cause: the farm's panel reads
**`Insufficient Seeds 0/5`** with Potato / Cover Crops / Wheat selected and a
Harvest of 179 Food; the **Seeds Depot beside it reads `180/180`**; the Drone
Hub covering it reads **24 drones, load `Low`**. So supply is not the
constraint — this is not a logistics starvation, it is a buffer that cannot
hold a tick's worth of work.

⚠️ **Cheats are enabled on this rig** (standing fact) and this colony is an
oversized speed-run. Neither touches the two numbers below, which are static
template data, but the *frequency* of the popup as experienced is a
playstyle-dependent observation, not a measurement.

## Question 1 — is the internal storage correct in code?

**It was never set.** `Data/BuildingTemplate/OpenFarm.lua` does not contain a
`consumption_max_storage` line at all, so the building takes the editor default
from the property definition:

```lua
{ template = true, category = "Resource Consumption", name = T(118, "Consumption Max Storage"),
  id = "consumption_max_storage", editor = "number", scale = const.ResourceScale,
  default = 5 * const.ResourceScale, ... }        -- Lua/HasConsumption.lua:44
```

⇒ **5000 raw = the `0/5` on the owner's screen.** `const.ResourceScale = 1000`
(`CommonLua/Libs/Resources/ResourcesFormatting.lua:6`).

## Question 2 — is the consumption correct in code?

**The consumption is the half that WAS deliberately tuned**, which is what makes
the pair look wrong rather than either number alone.

* **Cadence.** `VegetationPlant` declares `vegetation_interval` with
  `default = 20` at `scale = 10` (`TerraformingBuilding.lua:365`), and
  `VegetationPlant:Init` turns it into the tick length:
  `building_update_time = MulDivRound(self.vegetation_interval, const.HourDuration, 10)`
  (`:373-374`). **Open Farm overrides it to `3`** — `0.3` in-game hours per
  tick against the class default `2.0`, a deliberate **6.7×** speed-up.
* **Volume per tick.** `OpenFarmBase:TryPlantVegetation` runs
  `local iterations = 3 + self:Random(3)` (`:918`) — **3 to 5 hexes per tick** —
  and every placement runs `Consume_Internal(self.consumption_amount)` (`:450`).
* **Cost per hex.** `consumption_amount = MulDivRound(self.consumption_multiplier,
  veg.placement_consumption_amount, 100)` (`:459`), i.e. the crop's own value at
  the default multiplier of 100. The Vegetable presets (`Data/Vegetation.lua`):
  Herbs **100** · Rapeseed **100** · Spinach **200** · Wheat **400** ·
  **Potato 600**.
* **Refill rate.** A drone carries
  `(Resources[res].carry_amount or const.ResourceScale) * g_Consts.DroneResourceCarryAmount`
  (`Drone.lua:719`); `DroneResourceCarryAmount = 1` (`__const.lua:639-640`) and
  **no resource in the tree overrides `carry_amount`**, so one trip = **1000**.

⇒ **At Potato a single tick can cost 5 × 600 = 3000 against a 5000 buffer.**
The building can therefore never bank two full ticks, and needs ~3 drone
round-trips per 0.3 in-game hours just to break even. One tick of delivery lag
empties it, which raises the Consumption work-blocked reason
(`Building.lua:2726`) — and the flip repeats every 0.3 h, which is the popup
churn the owner is hearing.

## Question 3 — the comparison, and it is the decisive one

A sweep of **all 287 building templates** found **29 with a real
`consumption_resource_type`**. Ten leave `consumption_max_storage` at the
default. ⭐ **But Open Farm is the only one of those ten that also tuned its
consumption cadence** — everyone else who made the building drink faster also
made the cup bigger.

The like-for-like comparison the owner asked for is the other `VegetationPlant`
and the only other Seeds consumer in the game:

| | `ForestationPlant` | `OpenFarm` |
|---|---|---|
| `consumption_max_storage` | **10000** — set explicitly in its template | ⛔ **5000** — never set, editor default |
| `vegetation_interval` | **20** (class default) ⇒ tick = **2.0 h** | ⛔ **3** ⇒ tick = **0.3 h** |
| hexes planted per tick | **1** (2 under `Policy_ForestationEffort`) | ⛔ **3 + Random(3) = 3–5** |
| dearest crop | Broadleaf **500** | Potato **600** |
| peak drain | 500 / 2.0 h = **250/h** | 3000 / 0.3 h = **10 000/h** |
| **runway on a full buffer** | **≈ 40 in-game hours** | ⛔ **≈ 0.5 in-game hours** |

**≈ 80× less runway than its sibling**, and even Open Farm's *cheapest* crop
(Herbs or Rapeseed at 100) buys only ~3 hours — still ~13× short.

For scale against the rest of the game, the heavy industrial consumers all sit
at 10000–20000 (`ConcretePlant`, `GHGFactory`, the four WasteRock refineries at
20000 with 10000 per op, `ReconCenter` at 20000), and even the two smallest
consumers on the list — `ShopsFood_Small` at 4000 and the 6000 Workshops — drain
per *visit* or per *workshift*, not 3–5 times every 18 in-game minutes.

## ⭐ 2026-08-15 — HOW TO MEASURE IT (owner asked: can a ride-along agent see consumption and the out-of-resource calls live?)

**Yes to both, and the second one is better than polling — the events are
broadcast as engine messages, so the instrument subscribes instead of patching.**

### (a) The consumption is plain readable state

Every number in this entry is a live field on the object, readable exactly the
way the `C39` bracket read `GetWorkshiftPerformance` state:

| field | what it gives |
|---|---|
| `consumption_stored_resources` | the seed buffer right now (the `0/5` on screen ×1000) |
| `consumption_max_storage` | 5000, confirming the default at runtime rather than from source |
| `consumption_amount` | the current crop's per-hex cost after `consumption_multiplier` |
| `building_update_time` | the tick length in ms — confirms 0.3 h at runtime |
| `working`, `GetNotWorkingReason()` | whether it is stalled and on what reason id |

⇒ a sampler thread polling these on a fixed cadence gives a **drain curve**, and
with the game paused at one `GameTime` it gives the same class of frozen-clock
reading the C39 leg used. ⛔ Output must go through the TestKit's `Note`/ModLog
path — bare `print()` does **not** reach the log (recorded harness fact).

### (b) The out-of-resource event is a MESSAGE, not just a state

The popup the owner is hearing is the aggregated `NotWorkingBuildings`
notification, and buildings are added to and removed from it one at a time:

```lua
BaseBuilding:UpdateNotWorkingBuildingsNotification()          -- BaseBuilding.lua:137-139
  -> UpdateObjectInNotification(self, self:ShouldShowNotWorkingNotification(),
                                "NotWorkingBuildings", self:GetMap())
     -> AddObjectToNotification / RemoveObjectFromNotification
        -> OnMsg.AddNotificationObject(notification, obj, object_params)   -- NotificationUI.lua:209
        -> OnMsg.RemoveNotificationObject(notification, obj, object_params) -- NotificationUI.lua:220
```

⭐ **Both are `OnMsg` broadcasts**, so the probe just declares
`function OnMsg.AddNotificationObject(n, obj) ... end` in its own file and
counts, filtered on `n.id == "NotWorkingBuildings"` and
`IsKindOf(obj, "OpenFarmBase")`. **No wrapper, no patched method, no risk to a
vanilla path** — the cleanest instrument this project has available. The
add/remove pair also yields the **flap count** directly, which is the number the
owner's complaint is actually about.

### ⭐ (c) And the preset explains why it is LOUD, which no amount of buffer maths would have shown

`Data/NotificationPreset.lua:638-655`, `NotWorkingBuildings`:

* `VoicedText` = *"A building has stopped working"*, `FxAction = "UINotification"`
* **`VoicePerObject = true`** and **`VoiceCooldown = 60000`** ⇒
  `OnMsg.AddNotificationObject` calls `QueueVoice(obj, ...)` **keyed on the
  object** (`NotificationUI.lua:209-218`). The 60-second cooldown is therefore
  **per farm, not per notification** — so **N flapping Open Farms can produce N
  voice lines per 60 real seconds**. On a terraforming speed-run with many
  farms, that is the reported symptom exactly.
* `SuppressTime = 120000` + `Suppressable = true` is the *player-dismiss* path
  (`Notifications.lua:87,141-146`): dismissing suppresses the whole notification
  **type for 120 s**, then it returns. No `SuppressTimeAfterStart` is set.
* `UpdatePeriod = 40000`.
⇒ ⚠️ **The amplifier is `VoicePerObject`, not the buffer size.** Any remedy aimed
at the noise rather than the economy (shape 2 above) has to reckon with this
line, and this is a fact about the vanilla preset that applies to every
not-working building in the game, not just farms.

## ⚠️ 2026-08-15 — CORRECTION to this entry's own arithmetic, from the owner's second screenshot: the crops ROTATE, so the mean cost applies, not the maximum

The owner supplied a second Open Farm panel and the fixture facts (**two farms
only**, save `C47FARM`). Checking the crop names against the presets forced a
re-read of how a placement picks its crop, and **this entry's headline drain
figure was the worst case, not the operating case.** Corrected here rather than
left to the measurement leg to discover.

### What was wrong

`ThreeHarvestTypesBuilding:GetCurrentHarvestType`
(`ThreeHarvestTypesBuilding.lua:40-60`) advances `current_harvest_idx` with
`self.current_harvest_idx % 3 + 1` **on every call**, skipping empty slots and
slots whose requirements fail. `VegetationPlant:PlaceVegetation` calls it once
per placement (`TerraformingBuilding.lua:438`), so consecutive hexes **cycle
round-robin through the selected crops**.

⇒ **the per-hex cost is the MEAN of the selected crops, not the dearest one.**
The `5 × 600 = 3000` figure above only holds if **Potato is the only crop
selected**. That is a real worst case and stays on the record as one, but it is
not what the owner's farms are doing.

## ⚠️ 2026-08-15 — SECOND CORRECTION TO THIS ENTRY, MADE BEFORE THE RUN: the voice cooldown is keyed on the TEXT and on REAL time, not on the object — so the "N farms ⇒ N voice lines" reading in §(c) is REFUTED at source

Prompt 01 of the `c47-farm` chain asks the measuring leg to *"check whether
`QueueVoice`'s cooldown is real or game time and use the right clock"*. Checking
it answered a second question nobody had asked, and the answer overturns a claim
this entry already carried. **Recorded here, before any launch, so the run is not
credited with discovering it and the prediction below is written against the
corrected model.**

### What §(c) says, and what the code says

§(c) above: *"the 60-second cooldown is therefore **per farm, not per
notification** — so **N flapping Open Farms can produce N voice lines per 60 real
seconds**."*

`QueueVoice` (`CommonLua/Voice.lua:343-360`), the function
`OnMsg.AddNotificationObject` actually calls:

```lua
local voiced_text_id = (voiced_text or "") ~= "" and (TGetID(voiced_text) or ...) or ""
if unique == "text" and find(VoiceQueue, 2, voiced_text_id) then return end
...
local now = RealTime()
local cooldown = VoicesCooldown[voiced_text_id] or now
if cooldown > now then return end
VoicesCooldown[voiced_text_id] = (voice_cooldown or 0) > 0 and (now + voice_cooldown) or nil
```

* ⛔ **`VoicesCooldown` is indexed by `voiced_text_id`, not by the object.** The
  object is passed as `id` and is used for queue de-duplication and for
  `VoiceQueueRemove` — it never keys the cooldown.
* ⛔ **The clock is `RealTime()`**, i.e. real milliseconds. Not `GameTime()`.
  Answering prompt 01's question directly: **REAL time.**
* ⛔ **And there is a second, independent gate.** The preset does not set
  `VoiceUnique`, so it takes the class default **`"text"`**
  (`CommonLua/Libs/Notifications/NotificationPreset.lua:34`) — a queued voice for
  that text blocks any further one until it plays.
* `VoicePerObject = true` decides only *which handler runs* — the per-object one
  at `NotificationUI.lua:211` instead of the per-notification one at `:199` — and
  what is passed as `id`. It does not make the cooldown per object.

⇒ **"A building has stopped working" can be queued at most once per 60 REAL
seconds for the entire colony**, from any not-working building in the game, not
once per farm. With two farms the ceiling is not 2 voices per 60 s; it is **1**,
shared with every other stalled building on the map.

### What this changes, and what it does not

* ⛔ **Struck:** the §(c) sentence *"N flapping Open Farms can produce N voice
  lines per 60 real seconds"* and the *"on a terraforming speed-run with many
  farms, that is the reported symptom exactly"* that follows it. Both describe a
  per-object cooldown the code does not have.
* ⛔ **Struck:** the correction section's own *"the `VoicePerObject` amplifier is
  capped at 2 queued voices per 60 real seconds"*. The cap is **1**, colony-wide.
* ✅ **Strengthened, not weakened, is the conclusion those sentences were
  supporting:** the "endless" quality the owner reported **cannot** come from
  voice volume, because voice volume is capped at one line per real minute no
  matter how many buildings flap. It has to come from the **banner**, whose
  add/remove path has no cooldown at all. The leg's job of reporting banner
  churn and voice-eligible adds separately becomes more important, not less.
* ⚠️ **The real/game-time divergence is now the interesting term.** The drain is
  game-time driven; the voice gate is real-time driven. At `1x` a game hour is
  30 real seconds, so 60 s of cooldown is 2 game hours; at `5x` it is 10 game
  hours; at `20x`, 40. So the faster the owner plays, the *fewer* voice lines per
  game hour they hear and the *more* banner churn they see — which is the shape
  of the complaint.
* ✅ **Untouched:** every buffer, cadence and crop number above. This correction
  is entirely within §(c).

⭐ **This is source, not measurement, and the leg makes it measurable.** The
sampler prints the live key set of `VoicesCooldown` during the run: **one key for
the whole colony is the measurement; a key per farm would refute this reading.**
