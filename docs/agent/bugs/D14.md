### D14 - Clean stand-down for full-body replacements (P2) `[cand 2026-09-13: pack-wide measurement; no module individually assessed]`

**Owner, 2026-09-13:** *"the full body replacements bug me, I hate them because we
cannot guarantee we cleanly stand down."* This entry is the design record for that
concern. It is **not** a defect report against any one module.

#### Why a replacement cannot ride a vendor fix, and a wrapper can

A module that **delegates** - captures the shipped function and calls it - inherits
whatever the vendor later does to that body. A module that **replaces** the body pins
it at our copy forever: when the vendor repairs the defect, our stale copy lands on
top of their fix and reverts it. That is the F114-in-reverse shape `FIX_POLICY` §2a
names, and it is why §3a's layer ordering already puts *"patch a synchronous input,
keep vanilla's body"* first.

⭐ **The precedent is not hypothetical.** `tools/bodycheck.py`'s own header records
it: the 1.1.0 re-verification found 35 modules to retire and **32 were modules whose
defect the developers had fixed themselves while our self-check passed anyway**.

#### MEASURED, the pack as it stands 2026-09-13

| measure | count |
|---|---|
| `Code/Fix_*.lua` modules | 45 |
| delegate to the captured original (ride a vendor change) | **24** |
| do **not** delegate - candidate full replacements | **21** |
| carry a `Require` gate of any kind | 41 |
| carry a **behaviour probe** | 15 |
| carry a custom `test` | 8 |

⛔ **Those sets are not aligned, and the intersection is UNMEASURED.** A module that
replaces a body *and* gates only structurally will re-install its stale copy over a
vendor fix. How many of the 21 are in that position is exactly what this entry is
open to answer. Several may be legitimately un-wrappable (a `MsgReaction`, a preset
patch, a UI rebuild) or may carry a `test` that does the job.

#### The instrument gap is class c, NOT "did the body change"

**MEASURED:** `python tools/bodycheck.py` is at **full manifest coverage** - 132 rows
over 47 stamped modules, **0 modules carry no manifest**; 120 OK, 9 SRC-NONE, 2
NO-DEFECT, 1 DEFECT-GONE (F48's paren, already triaged in `90_SaveSanitizer`). Its
post-patch run is binding procedure (`WORKFLOW.md:171`).

⇒ **"Did the code we replaced change?" is already answered, at the desk, with no game
launch.** The gap is the blind spot bodycheck itself declares:

> It does **NOT** see semantics moving under a wrapper (class c), and it sees nothing
> at all for a NO-DEFECT or NO-MANIFEST module.

So a vendor can repair a defect **without touching the body we pinned** - a callee
changes, a preset value moves, a modifier is applied elsewhere - and every instrument
the pack owns still reads GREEN. That is the live exposure.

#### Why a runtime body check cannot close it

**MEASURED, our own logs:** `[SMRTest] no debug.getinfo (mod sandbox)`. Body or
bytecode inspection is unavailable from inside a mod, so the answer cannot be "compare
the body at runtime". It has to be a **behaviour** probe: *does the defect still
reproduce?* That is the same instrument `FIX_POLICY` §2a already requires for a
runtime decline, which is the second thing this buys.

#### Control - what would falsify the concern

For any module claimed exposed, the falsifier is a **class-c case**: a change to
shipped data or a callee that removes the defect while leaving our pinned body
byte-identical, with `bodycheck` still reporting OK. If no such case can be
constructed for a module, that module is not class-c exposed and the entry says so.
⛔ A module being a full replacement is **not by itself** a finding.

#### Not opened

The per-module intersection of "replaces a body" with "gate detects a fix rather than
a restructure" - the whole of the work · whether any of the 21 could be re-shaped as a
delegating wrapper without changing behaviour · whether the TestKit's 97 probes
already cover any of these cases · whether a class-c probe can be written at all for
a data-patch module, where there is no body and no call to observe · the cost of
running the resulting probe set on every game patch.
